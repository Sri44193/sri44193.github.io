The big picture

The whole chapter on one page. Come back here after each topic.

📌 The chapter in one sentence A multimodal chatbot must do more than sound fluent: it must stay grounded in images, transcripts and records across many turns. Evaluate it through a 10-phase lifecycle (data → pretraining → instruction tuning → fine-tuning → robustness → inference safeguards → decoding stability → long-conversation drift → cost → user outcomes), and turn every KPI into a measurable instrument with a data source, scoring rule, threshold and escalation.
🧪 Why a QA lead should care This chapter is a giant KPI catalogue. Don't memorise names; learn the patterns: per-slice reporting, ablations, hard negatives, calibration, multi-turn drift tests and fallback quality. They apply to any conversational AI you'll test.

Architecture of multimodal conversational LLMs

Encoders per modality, one shared backbone 📖 PDF p. 243–250

✍️ Text

Queries, dialogue, documents, logs. Input and output.

🖼️ Image

Photos, scans, screenshots; encoded by CNNs or vision transformers.

🎙️ Audio

Speech is often transcribed first; adds timing and tone (prosody).

🎞️ Video

Images over time: motion and events.

🗃️ Structured data

Tables, records, sensor readings, knowledge graphs.

Examples the book names include GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude 3 / Claude Vision, Llama 3.2 Vision, LLaVA, BLIP-2, Flamingo, Qwen-VL; the list changes fast.

Fusion strategyHowTest it for…
Early fusionVisual embeddings injected as pseudo-tokens at the inputVisual token dilution: in long chats the model leans on text history and forgets the image
Late fusionModalities processed separately, combined near the outputModality siloing: weak integration between image and text
InterleavedVisual and text tokens mixed within the transformer stackAlignment precision vs compute cost
📌 Remember The fusion design decides what to test. Conversational systems also need dialogue state: earlier turns, instructions and references to images must stay consistently grounded. Token compression and pooling keep latency manageable but can drop detail.

3

Data quality (e-commerce example)

Text, vision and cross-modal quality 📖 PDF p. 254–257

🧩 The scenario A shopping assistant: a customer uploads a photo of a jacket, says "similar but cheaper, in navy", and the bot combines catalogue images, product data, earlier turns and the upload. A wrong colour label or a mismatched image directly causes wrong recommendations, returns and lost trust.

✍️ Text quality

Accuracy · completeness (material, size, compatibility) · consistent terms and schema · correct intent and entity labels · linguistic diversity.

🖼️ Vision quality

Clarity · correct labels · attributes that match what's visible · each image tied to the right product.

🔗 Cross-modal quality

Descriptions match the picture; conversations reference the right image. Misalignment → hallucinated answers.

ToolGood forCan't do
CleanlabLikely noisy labelsTell "formal shoe" from "office shoe"
Great ExpectationsSchema rules, required fieldsJudge whether a description matches the image
CleanVisionBlur, corruption, duplicates, low resolutionSemantic correctness
FiftyOneExploring clusters, outliers, label distributionsDecide if an outlier is a real problem
CLIP similarityBroad image–text mismatchExact shade, texture, variant, catalogue jargon (it reflects its own web training)

Also: Hugging Face Datasets and DataPrep for profiling, CVAT analytics for annotation consistency.

⚠️ Tools give false confidence Hard labels (SKU match, missing fields, duplicates, resolution) suit automation. Fuzzy labels (style, perceived colour, "is this a good substitute?", user intent) need human calibration. Always hand-audit CLIP's low-confidence and borderline pairs.
4

Coverage and data readiness

Check the data before training, not after 📖 PDF p. 257–259

🧒 Coverage dimensions Product categories (apparel, electronics, home) · attributes (size, colour, material, specs) · visual diversity (lighting, angles, backgrounds, packaging) · conversational diversity (phrasing, negotiation, clarification, sentiment) · intents (search, compare, complain, recommend, after-sales) · rare products and edge cases. Without coverage the model defaults to dominant patterns.
🧒 Readiness checks Distribution of categories and intents · attribute frequencies · missing data · class imbalance · embedding clusters (gaps), outliers (mislabels), image–text similarity · sampled annotation audits with the right agreement metric:
SituationAgreement metric
Quick categorical checkPercent agreement (overstates when labels are imbalanced)
Two annotators, categoriesCohen's kappa
More than two annotators, categoriesFleiss' kappa
Ordinal, missing or mixed labelsKrippendorff's alpha (start at ≥ 0.67, subject to domain review)
Free text (descriptions, explanations)Rubric-based human review + semantic similarity
➕ Why percent agreement misleads If 95% of items are "not defective", two annotators who both say "not defective" every time agree 95%, yet they never found a defect. Kappa-style metrics correct for agreement expected by chance.
5

Data KPIs linked to business KPIs

Traceability from dataset to revenue 📖 PDF p. 259–260

💎 Data quality KPIs

Attribute accuracy rate · image–text alignment score · annotation agreement · missing metadata ratio · image quality score.

🗺️ Coverage KPIs

Category coverage ratio · attribute coverage distribution · intent distribution · rare-case representation index · linguistic diversity.

💰 Business KPIs

Conversational success rate · grounded response accuracy · hallucination rate · user friction index (clarifications, backtracking) · conversion and order value · returns and complaints.

📌 The point Better alignment and coverage should show up as fewer hallucinations and more successful conversations. If data KPIs improve but business KPIs don't move, you're measuring the wrong thing.
6

Pretraining alignment (medical example)

Are speech, images and text linked meaningfully? 📖 PDF p. 261–265

🧒 Plain English From here the example switches to a medical assistant: X-rays, pathology slides, skin photos, clinical notes and dictated questions. Pretraining objectives (contrastive image–text, image-conditioned masked language modelling, matching, joint generation) must produce embeddings where findings and descriptions line up.

🔎 Cross-modal retrieval

Given a chest X-ray, retrieve the right report (and vice versa). Accuracy at top-1, top-5, top-10.

🪤 Hard negatives

Compare true pairs not just with random pairs but with similar-but-different ones (same scan type, different diagnosis). AUROC of matched vs mismatched.

🎭 Masked terms

Hide diagnostic words in a report; does the image help recover them? Measure perplexity reduction vs a text-only baseline, and check attribution, since closed vocabularies reward guessing.

🎙️ Speech

Transcript-only checks miss hesitation, urgency and tone. Test acoustics too, or say clearly you only test transcripts. False alignment rate (claiming the image supports a finding it doesn't) is safety-critical.

📌 Other pretraining checks Medical VQA benchmarks · calibration (expected calibration error, Brier score) · fairness by age, sex and imaging device · modality balance (attention entropy, attribution) · perturbation stability · rare disease retrieval with macro-averaged metrics · consistency across follow-up scans over time.
➕ Calibration in one line A model is calibrated if, of all the answers it gives with "80% confidence", about 80% are right. Expected calibration error measures the gap; the Brier score is the mean squared error of predicted probabilities.
7

Instruction and modality alignment tuning

Follow the clinician's directive, stay grounded 📖 PDF p. 265–270

🧩 Example "Give a ranked list of three likely diagnoses from this chest CT and report." The response must contain exactly three, in that format (structural adherence), answer the actual question (directive accuracy), and add nothing irrelevant (deviation rate).

📋 Adherence

Instruction adherence rate · format compliance · structural adherence · directive accuracy · deviation rate.

🎯 Grounding

Visual grounding precision (claims verifiable in the image) · attribution fidelity (heat map overlap with annotated lesions).

👻 Hallucination

Use normal scans as negative cases to get the false-positive diagnosis rate · unsupported assertion rate.

⚔️ Contradictions

"No fracture visible" but "fracture diagnosed" = critical misalignment.

💬 Multi-turn tracking

Instruction retention and behavioural compliance: still avoiding jargon five turns after being asked for patient-friendly language.

⚖️ Balance and fairness

Ablation (remove the image: does performance stay the same? then it's ignoring it) · adherence by demographic group.

🧪 Adversarial tests at this stage Refusal appropriateness for disallowed advice · indirect prompt injection hidden in clinical notes, structured fields, OCR text, image metadata or attached reports · multi-turn manipulation that drifts gradually towards unsafe output · safe fallback ("consult a clinician") when uncertain. Also track the latency cost of alignment.
8

Task-specific fine-tuning

Radiology, dermatology, pathology workflows 📖 PDF p. 270–275

🩻 Radiology

Sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV per pathology · macro F1 · clinically significant error rate (errors that would change patient management) · report completeness and template adherence · ambiguity rate.

🩹 Dermatology triage

Sensitivity for melanoma at a specificity floor agreed with experts in advance · lesion features (asymmetry, border, colour, diameter) · over-reassurance rate.

🔬 Pathology

Feature localisation · differential-diagnosis ranking vs expert consensus · diagnostic calibration.

⚠️ Sensitivity alone is meaningless Report sensitivity, specificity and the decision threshold together. You can get 100% sensitivity by flagging everyone. ➕ Same rule for any classifier you test: a recall number without the threshold and precision isn't a result.
📌 Cross-cutting KPIs Multi-turn: context retention, inconsistency rate, task persistence · generalisation by demographics, device and hospital · rare-condition recall · unsupported finding rate and evidence citation accuracy · clinician-rated usefulness and actionability · degradation slope under poor images · safe uncertainty expression · efficiency · an error taxonomy (perception, reasoning, instruction misreading, memory) · drift and forgetting over updates · EHR data compatibility.

Generalisation and robustness across modalities

Does it hold up outside the training conditions? 📖 PDF p. 275–280

🏥 Distribution shift

Cross-site accuracy on hospitals not in training; the in- vs out-of-distribution gap.

⚖️ Fairness

Accuracy by age, sex, ethnicity, comorbidity; equalised false-negative and false-positive rates.

🧩 Rare conditions

Rare-disease recall, macro F1, long-tail robustness.

❓ Out-of-distribution detection

Unfamiliar cases should raise uncertainty, not confident errors. Overconfidence under shift is safety-critical.

🔄 Counterfactual fairness

Change only the demographic words in the history, keep the image; the diagnosis shouldn't change.

⏳ Temporal and continual

Stable findings across serial scans (false progression rate); no catastrophic forgetting after updates.

⚙️ Modality dropout, done properly 
score_full   ← model(text, real image)
score_none   ← model(text, no image)
score_blank  ← model(text, blank image)
score_noise  ← model(text, noise image)
if score_blank ≈ score_full or score_noise ≈ score_full:
    model needs visual tokens present, but isn't reading them   # shortcut
if score_none ≈ score_full:
    model over-relies on text priors                         # weak grounding
📌 Also Perturbation degradation slope (noise, brightness, compression, occlusion) · paraphrase and transcription-error stability · contextual stability over long dialogues (change hypotheses only with new evidence) · missing metadata and partial images (graceful degradation) · stability under load · consistent explanations across runs.
10

Input validation and context handling at inference

Catch bad inputs; keep the conversation straight 📖 PDF p. 280–284

🖼️ Image quality

Rejection accuracy for non-diagnostic images. False acceptance (bad image treated as fine) = high risk; false rejection slows workflow.

🏷️ Metadata

Detect missing fields and contradictions: metadata says chest X-ray, the image looks abdominal → flag.

❓ Ambiguous queries

Ask for clarification instead of assuming (clarification initiation frequency).

🧠 Context

Keep established findings (a nodule found in turn 2 stays found) · contextual contradiction rate · entity tracking and coreference · new images bound to the right turn.

🧒 When the conversation gets too long Document the strategy (drop oldest turns, summarise, compress history, retrieve key facts) and test that it keeps clinically critical information (the original image evidence, established findings, prior diagnoses, safety constraints) while dropping filler. Measure an information retention score.
🧪 Testing angle Also measure prompt-injection resistance in text inputs, malformed-file detection, validation overhead vs total latency, input traceability (which inputs influenced this output?) and recovery success after a fallback.
11

Decoding stability and cross-modal reasoning at runtime

Same input, same diagnosis 📖 PDF p. 285–288

🎲 Stability

Response variance index · clinical conclusion consistency across repeated runs · greedy vs sampling comparison · ensemble agreement across seeds.

⚔️ Coherence

"Clear lung fields" + "pneumonia" = cross-modal contradiction. Visual evidence citation accuracy; attribution overlap with expert regions.

🔗 Reasoning chains

Do intermediate steps stay consistent across runs and actually support the conclusion?

🎚️ Parameter sensitivity

Temperature sensitivity slope, beam width impact. Meaningful variation should be release-blocking, not just reported.

📌 Also watch Runtime hallucination on normal cases · cross-turn drift without new evidence · calibration and overconfidence under sampling · accuracy vs latency trade-off when decoding is sped up · uncertainty on ambiguous images · anomaly alerts (sensitivity and false-alarm rate) · reproducible explanations.

Transcription drift and reasoning degradation

Long conversations slowly lose the plot 📖 PDF p. 289–292

🧒 Plain English Transcription drift: small dictation errors enter the conversation memory and compound. Reasoning degradation: logic and grounding weaken as the dialogue grows. Word error rate alone isn't enough; measure whether clinical meaning and key terms survive transcription.

🧪 Dialogue extension tests

Build long synthetic chats with little new information; does the model keep earlier findings (context stability, memory interference)?

🔁 Re-grounding

Periodically ask it to restate the key visual findings; compare with the original image. Does it notice its own contradictions?

📈 Hallucination growth

Small unsupported claims grow into false stories. Does it retract when challenged?

📏 Length stress

Maximum stable turn threshold and early degradation onset: at which turn does accuracy start to fall?

📌 Also Unjustified escalation rate (benign → malignant without new data) · confidence drift · chronological confusion between scan dates · and evaluate the drift monitors themselves: alert precision, false alarm rate, recall against annotated drift points.
13

End-to-end latency, throughput and cost

Accurate isn't enough if it's too slow or too dear 📖 PDF p. 293–296

⏱️ Latency

Median and p95; compliance with a maximum allowed time. Break it down: preprocessing, inference, post-processing. Watch variance across case complexity.

📈 Throughput and scale

Maximum sustainable throughput while accuracy holds · degradation slope under load · horizontal and vertical scaling.

💰 Cost

Cost per consultation · cost–accuracy curve · marginal cost efficiency · energy and carbon per thousand consultations.

🏥 Real-world operations

Resource-limited sites · queue wait at peak, priority handling for urgent cases, overflow · EHR and imaging-archive integration · uptime and recovery · encryption overhead.

📌 Value, not just cost Productivity gain (clinician time saved), cost–benefit vs labour, ROI. And perceived speed: within the technical limit can still feel slow to clinicians.
14

Conversational success, user friction and fallback

Did the user actually get what they needed, safely? 📖 PDF p. 297–300

✅ Success

Task completion rate (no escalation or abandonment) · task-aligned accuracy · grounded response proportion · unsupported claim frequency.

🧱 Friction

Clarification frequency · repetition rate · turn inefficiency (turns to resolve vs benchmark) · misinterpretation before clarifying.

🤝 Trust

Clinician trust index · patient comprehension · explanation usefulness.

🛟 Fallback

Safe escalation rate vs inappropriate autonomy rate: defer neither too much nor too little. Clear uncertainty wording.

📌 Also Safety-event and near-miss rates, in-conversation correction · turns and time to resolution · repeat use and abandonment · fairness of success and fallback across groups · feedback integration into updates · audit-ready logs, transparency and traceability.

From KPI name to real measurement

A KPI without a protocol is just a word 📖 PDF p. 301–303

🧒 Plain English The lifecycle gives the structure; the KPIs say what to measure. Before evaluation starts, every KPI needs six things. Thresholds must come from clinical, business or operational risk review (pilot data, expert baselines, acceptable failure limits), not copied across domains. 
kpi: visual_grounding_precision           # my illustrative example
data_source: 300 expert-labelled chest X-rays, stratified by site and age
annotation: radiologist marks regions supporting each finding
scoring_rule: share of claimed findings whose cited region overlaps the label
threshold: set in risk review from a pilot (e.g. >= 0.90 overall, no slice < 0.85)
review_process: second reader on disagreements; monthly calibration
escalation: below threshold on any slice -> block release, open investigation
🧪 Testing angle Turn the chapter into a spreadsheet: one row per KPI you actually need, with those six columns filled in. Anything you can't fill in isn't ready to be a release gate.
❓ Check yourself
A model's accuracy is the same with the real image and with a blank image. What does that tell you?
It isn't reading the visual content; it relies on text priors or simply the presence of visual tokens. Grounding is weak.
Why use hard negatives in retrieval tests?
Random mismatches are easy to tell apart. Similar-but-different pairs (same scan type, different diagnosis) show whether the model separates clinically meaningful differences.
A triage model reports 98% sensitivity for melanoma. What's missing?
The specificity and the decision threshold. Without them, 98% sensitivity could mean referring almost everyone.

✅ One-page cheat sheet

  1. 🧩 Encoders per modality + shared backbone. Early fusion → test visual dilution in long chats; late fusion → test siloing.
  2. 🔟 Lifecycle: KPIs → data → pretraining → instruction tuning → fine-tuning → robustness → inference safeguards → decoding → cost → outcomes.
  3. 💎 Data quality: text, vision, cross-modal. Tools help with hard labels; fuzzy labels need humans. Audit CLIP borderline pairs.
  4. 🗺️ Coverage across categories, attributes, visuals, conversations, intents, rare cases. Kappa / Krippendorff's alpha (≥ 0.67 start).
  5. 🔗 Link data KPIs to business KPIs (success, friction, hallucination, conversion, returns).
  6. 🧠 Pretraining: retrieval top-k, hard negatives, masked terms vs text-only baseline, speech beyond transcripts, calibration, fairness, rare cases.
  7. 📋 Instruction tuning: adherence, grounding precision, negative cases, contradictions, multi-turn compliance, indirect injection, gradual manipulation.
  8. 🩻 Fine-tuning: sensitivity + specificity + threshold together; clinically significant error rate; error taxonomy.
  9. 🌐 Robustness: cross-site, fairness, rare recall, OOD uncertainty, counterfactual fairness, blank/noise ablations.
  10. 🛂 Inference: false acceptance of bad images, metadata contradictions, clarification, context retention strategy, traceability.
  11. 🎲 Decoding: conclusion consistency across runs; parameter sensitivity can block release.
  12. 🧵 Long chats: drift propagation, re-grounding, hallucination growth, maximum stable turns.
  13. ⏱️ Latency breakdown, throughput while accurate, cost per consultation, ROI, perceived speed.
  14. 🛟 Outcomes: completion, friction, trust, safe escalation vs inappropriate autonomy, fairness.
  15. 📏 Every KPI: data source, annotation, scoring rule, threshold, review, escalation.

Try it: a mini lab

Test a multimodal chatbot over a long conversation

  1. Upload a product photo to a multimodal assistant and state a fact about it in turn 1 (e.g. "it's size M").
  2. Have 15 turns of filler chat, then ask it to restate the image details and the size. Note the first turn where anything drifts.
  3. Repeat with the image replaced by a blank image. Do the answers change much?
  4. Ask the same question 5 times in fresh chats. Compute conclusion consistency.
  5. Pick 3 KPIs from this chapter and fill in the six-field measurement template for each.
