The big picture

The whole chapter on one page. Come back here after each topic.

📌 The chapter in one sentence In production there are no bounding-box labels, so grounding must be inferred from runtime evidence: every stage of the VLM pipeline gets a control that produces a metric, and every load-bearing metric triggers an action: accept, abstain, escalate, fall back, open an incident or roll back, field by field.
📌 The five principles the chapter keeps returning to 1. The submitted image is not necessarily the encoded image. · 2. Structural validity is not groundedness. · 3. Confidence is not evidence. · 4. Safety failures are not compensated by quality gains. · 5. Every load-bearing metric must trigger a defined action.

From checkpoint evidence to runtime control

The gold set doesn't come to production 📖 PDF p. 196–199

🧒 Plain English Chapter 4's thresholds, slices and baselines travel into production, but the gold set doesn't: branch-office invoices arrive with no boxes and no ground truth. Two new problems appear: input shift (new scanners, layouts, capture conditions) and silent grounding failure (answers on images nobody can check against a reference).
MomentEvidence availableDecision
Training checkpointGold labels, controlled slices, fixed harness, threshold cardsAdvance, hold, diagnose
Pre-deploymentRelease metrics, safety gates, efficiency budgetsApprove, block, narrow the release
Inference (live)Trace fields, metadata, evidence flags, sampled graders, downstream rejections, drift signalsAccept, abstain, escalate, roll back, open an incident
📌 Three VLM-only control surfaces Every text control from Chapter 3 still applies, plus three with no text equivalent: the gap between the submitted and encoded image · evidence binding to an image region (not just a tool success flag) · a degraded-input policy (when a poor image is accepted, flagged, reviewed or refused).

3

Image intake and input validation

Never invoke the model under uncertainty 📖 PDF p. 203–207

🧒 Plain English An image carries much more than pixels: format and codec, colour space, EXIF metadata, page count, ICC profile, DPI, capture source (scanner vs phone vs rendered). Plus request metadata (customer tier, document-type hint, locale, consumer). Each can fail. Validate hints against the image itself: a "standard invoice" hint on what looks like a handwritten receipt is unsafe to pass on.

📦 Format

Check MIME types and codecs; verify by actually decoding, not by reading the header; cap size and page count.

📏 Resolution

DPI and pixel bounds per tier. Outside them: reject with a reason code, downsample with a tag, or escalate. Always recorded.

📷 Capture quality

Cheap deterministic detectors: blur (frequency or edge sharpness), exposure and contrast, skew, page completeness. Version them like the model.

🎨 Colour space

CMYK→RGB, alpha, greyscale. Unsupported profiles → reject or escalate, never silently continue.

🔒 Privacy and safety

Strip GPS and device IDs from EXIF. Safety pre-screen (adversarial, not structural). Redaction of sensitive regions is mandatory where required; ROI cropping is optional.

🧩 A silent failure (the book's example) A CMYK→RGB conversion lowers contrast on a pale receipt. A human can still read the small numbers; the model's OCR and patch recognition can't.
Intake failureActionTrace field
Decode failure / unsupported formatReject with reason codedecode_status, mime_type, codec
Missing required metadataEscalate or ask to resubmitmissing_metadata_fields
Near-limit resolution or skewDegraded fallback or human reviewresolution_tier, skew_score
Safety pre-screen hitReject or safety escalationsafety_prescreen_label
Redaction requiredRedact, tag, recheck extraction impactredaction_policy_version

The book's example values for a tier-one invoice workflow. Real thresholds come from your capture-quality strata, variance, error cost and review capacity.

4

Visual context: what the model actually sees

The submitted image is not the encoded image 📖 PDF p. 207–209

🧒 Plain English Between intake and the encoder, the image is resized, tiled, colour-adjusted and maybe recompressed, often by the serving stack without telling anyone. Downsampling can blur small numbers before the model ever "reads" them.
⚙️ Patch maths: why resolution costs so much 
patches per side = image side ÷ patch size
336 × 336 image, 14 × 14 patches → 24 × 24 = 576 image tokens
672 × 672 image, 14 × 14 patches → 48 × 48 = 2,304 image tokens
# doubling the side quadruples the tokens: small fields get readable, latency and cost go up
⚠️ Silent transformations Serving frameworks may apply gamma correction, contrast normalisation or channel reordering, or recompress images, with no errors and no logs. Log format, byte size, resolution tier, colour space and a hash at each serving boundary. 
encoder input trace (per request):
  post_preprocess_hash: sha256 of the image bytes the encoder actually received
  preprocess_chain:
    version, operations, resolution_policy, color_space_policy,
    tiling_policy, ocr_engine_version, coordinate_space
📌 Multi-page and tiling Preserve page order, image role and page IDs end to end. Every claim needs page_id, field_name, value, bbox, coordinate_space, evidence_source. Block automatic acceptance of cross-page claims (totals over several pages) unless every supporting page and region is named. Watch tile_boundary_field_failure_rate: fields sitting on a tile edge that fail checks.
🧪 Testing angle Feed the same invoice through the whole serving stack and compare the logged hash with what you expected to send. Then test fields placed exactly on tile boundaries.
5

Prompt governance for multimodal inputs

Schema and layout-hint edits are high risk 📖 PDF p. 209–210

🧒 Plain English A VLM prompt = system instruction + task + output schema (with evidence subfields: boxes, region citations, confidence) + optional layout hints + optional image-text examples + the convention for referencing the image. All versioned together.
🔴 Schema editsRename a field without updating the AP integration → silent "missing data".
🔴 Layout hints"Total is bottom-right" fails for a vendor who puts it top-right.
🔴 Evidence formatsChanging box, citation or confidence format can make the gate misread evidence.
🟠 Example image swapsExamples calibrate expectations; a swap can regress other inputs.
📌 Log on every request Prompt ID, version and hash · schema version · image-reference convention · preprocessing version · encoder and projection versions. Otherwise you can't tell an encoder upgrade from a prompt change. High-risk edits must pass full grounding regression on non-standard layouts, phone receipts and multi-page contracts too.

Grounded output contracts

Every field says where it came from 📖 PDF p. 210–212

🧒 Plain English The base contract: fields present, right types, allowed values. The grounded extension: each field also carries a bounding box, a region citation (in post-preprocessing coordinates), a calibrated confidence and an evidence-source flag.
Evidence sourceMeaningAP handlingOn failure
🖼️ Visual regionGrounded to a cited regionAccept if thresholds passEscalate if region missing or wrong
🔤 OCROCR over the cited region supports itAccept or reconcile by field tierHuman review of tier-1 disagreement
🧮 DerivedComputed from other grounded fieldsAccept if inputs verifiedBlock if sources unverified
🧠 Model-internalNo explicit visual anchorHuman review; never auto-writtenAbstain on high-impact numbers

 "bbox": [420, 1810, 760, 1870], "coordinate_space": "post_preprocess_image",
 "confidence": 0.97, "evidence_source": "visual_region+ocr",
 "ocr_value": "12,847.50", "ocr_agreement": true, "decision": "accept"}

🧱 Structural failure

total_due missing.

📐 Geometric failure

Bounding box outside the image.

🧮 Consistency failure

Line items don't sum to the subtotal within the document's rounding rule.

📌 Remember A passing contract proves usability, not correct values, correct regions or real grounding. Report them separately. A drop in contract_pass_rate is a leading indicator: grounding metrics often move next.

Decoding policy and stability

Stable, by field type 📖 PDF p. 213–215

🧒 Plain English Two sources of variance: decoding randomness (temperature, top-p, top-k, sampling vs greedy) and visual-input variance: kernels, batching, preprocessing and numerical precision can change outputs even with identical settings. Verify stability empirically before trusting it for tier-one decisions.
Field typeDecodingWhy
🔢 Numeric (totals, tax, dates)Temperature 0.0 / greedyVariance changes payments
📦 Boxes and evidence fieldsTemperature 0.0The evidence gate depends on them
✍️ Free text0.1, only with a verified normaliserSmall wording changes are absorbed by entity resolution

If per-field decoding isn't possible, use the strictest setting for the whole output, or split passes when budgets allow.

🧩 Near the gate = route to review (the book's example) Acceptance needs confidence ≥ 0.95, but repeated runs give 0.92–0.97. The field is too close to the gate: send to review or tighten controls. For high-risk requests, run extraction several times and escalate if runs disagree: disagreement shows the input is at the edge of reliable operation.
⚠️ Stably wrong A system can give the same wrong total every time with a passing contract. Stability isn't correctness; that's what runtime grounding evaluation is for.
8

Runtime grounding evaluation without labels

Leading and lagging signals, graders and humans 📖 PDF p. 215–219

⏩ Leading signals

Contract pass rate, structural sanity of boxes, confidence distribution drift: they move before users see failures.

⏪ Lagging signal

Downstream AP rejection: valuable, but only after the output was emitted.

🔀 Cross-modality check

OCR over the cited region vs the model's value. Disagreement goes to the evidence gate, never silently resolved.

👩 Humans (~8%)High-value invoices, phone receipts, unusual layouts, low confidence, near-threshold, downstream-rejected.
🤖 VLM grader (~5%)Stratified by document type, capture quality, vendor and layout.
✅ Deterministic + OCREvery request, every numeric field.

Illustrative rates from the book's blueprint; recalibrate from volume, disagreement, staffing and risk.

⚠️ Grader blind spots grow at runtime A grader that runs continuously and shares the model's habits will systematically miss them. Use a different model family or prove calibration against humans on your domain; re-run side-by-side scoring when the grader is updated. Keep reviewers calibrated too (image first, double-scoring, inter-rater checks); a change in the reviewer pool can shift scores even when the model hasn't changed.

The evidence-binding gate

The most important single control in a tier-one VLM 📖 PDF p. 219–222

🧒 Plain English Sampling catches failures on average; the gate stops a specific high-impact claim. Four obligations: the value is derivable from a region · the region exists · the region's content matches the value · the output reflects the evidence state, not just the model's confidence. Most systems fail at the fourth: high confidence gets forwarded unchecked. The execution flow sets evidence flags, not the model.
⚙️ The conjunctive gate for tier-one numeric fields 
has_region ← field has a bounding box
ocr_agrees ← |normalise(value) − normalise(ocr_value)| ≤ 0.01
              # normalise: strip currency, handle separators, locale decimals, rounding
confident  ← confidence ≥ 0.95
if has_region and ocr_agrees and confident:  accept
elif not has_region and not ocr_agrees:    abstain
else:                                       human review
# ALL three must hold: a weighted score could hide a missing source behind two strong ones
SignalTier-1 numericTier-2 free text
Cross-source disagreement (model ≠ OCR)Abstain / escalateEscalate
Confidence anomaly (high confidence on a header region)EscalateRepair
Late-position hallucination in long outputsAbstain / escalateRepair

Several signals at once → the most restrictive response. Repair is bounded by the retry budget.

⚠️ Why no "repair" for tier-1 disagreements If the model and OCR disagree, re-running until a model output happens to match OCR doesn't resolve the conflict: it launders it into an accepted value. Only abstain or escalate.
📌 The consumer must honour the gate unsupported_high_impact_claim_rate target ≈ 0; any non-zero value blocks promotion or opens an incident. And if the AP system writes every field regardless of flags, the gate is useless: evidence binding is a contract between the VLM and its consumer.

Groundedness at runtime: coverage, faithfulness, localisation

The image is the "retrieved context" 📖 PDF p. 222–226

👀 Visual coverage

Did it look at the right regions? A "total" box over the company logo is a coverage failure.

🤝 Visual faithfulness

Do claims match what's in the cited regions (OCR, detectors, sampled graders)?

📍 Localisation correctness

A valid box on the wrong region (line-item text instead of tax) passes the contract but fails here.

🧒 Layout grounding checks (cheap and deterministic) Is the value near its label? Are table rows in increasing order and columns aligned? Do section claims sit below their header? Is the total line positioned correctly relative to subtotal and tax? If retrieval is added, measure image grounding and retrieval grounding separately.
DriftSignalLikely causeAction
CoverageLess overlap with expected regionsNew layout, or prompt hints no longer matchRefresh slice data or the prompt directive
FaithfulnessMore OCR/grader disagreementOCR change, scanner quality, model regressionCheck OCR version trace; route affected fields
LocalisationValid boxes on wrong regionsTemplate shift, tiling failure, encoder or resolution changeUpdate layout checks and tiling
ConsumerAP rejections change, VLM unchangedDownstream acceptance rule changedVersion consumer policy; recalibrate gates
📌 Degraded inputs get a policy per quality stratum Full automation · reduced-confidence output · mandatory human review · refusal. A runtime stress suite of real production samples per stratum checks performance; when a stratum drifts below threshold, route it to human review until fixed.

Multimodal safety at runtime: canaries and slices

The training safety gate becomes a live obligation 📖 PDF p. 226–228

🧒 Plain English Canary probes = known adversarial images slipped into live traffic at a set rate, looking like real requests. Their outputs are intercepted before the AP system and checked. A periodic review finds a regression after two weeks of exposure; continuous canaries find it in hours.

🚫 Refusal canaries

A drop in correct refusals is an immediate alert.

✍️ Override resistance

Intake detection of visible instructions, then canaries checking the model still resists. New attacks become new probes at once.

💉 Injection canaries

Any non-zero success is a hard incident, not a metric to tune. Also cover low-visibility payloads: steganography, typographic patches, encoder-targeted perturbations.

Safety sliceWhy riskierPolicy
📱 Phone-captured docsBlur, lighting, ambiguous embedded textStricter pre-screen, lower escalation threshold, targeted canaries
🧾 Non-standard layoutsLess layout certainty → more injection exposureSlice floor, human review of boundary cases
💰 High-value documentsHigher consequence, not higher frequencyTier-one floor; any canary failure = hard blocker
📝 Dense embedded textMore override and injection riskDedicated canary set
✂️ Redaction/ROI complexityCropping can remove labels and table contextCheck extraction impact after redaction
📌 Why runtime safety still matters New attacks appear on live traffic; small updates (LoRA, projection retraining) can shift refusals too little for training gates but enough to matter; and some images are only unsafe in combination with a particular prompt. The canary catalogue is a versioned artifact that grows with the threat surface.

Budgets at production resolutions

Resolution is both a quality lever and a cost lever 📖 PDF p. 229–231

DialBoundsVLM twistIf breached
🔤 Token + image tokenPer-request input costImage tokens grow with resolution²; watch the image:text token ratioInvestigate resolution or traffic shift
🔁 RetryRetries per requestContract repair, grader retry, re-encodingPrioritise re-encoding; cap repairs
⏱️ TimeoutStage latencyEncoder p99 dominates high-resolution requestsFallback, truncate or review
⚖️ GraderEvaluation spendCalibrated grader rate on live strataReduce sampling or rebalance
📉 ErrorReliability over a windowBurn from validation, evidence, grounding, safety eventsRollback during canary; incident in production
📌 Rules Tier-one numeric fields get full resolution; only non-critical regions may be downsampled, with tags and per-stratum monitoring. Re-encoding retries beat grader retries (encoder failures hit users; grader failures only hit sampling). Hard blockers (safety canary failure, unsupported high-impact claim) skip "gradual burn" and go straight to rollback or incident. Segment p95/p99 by resolution, page count, document type, capture source and branch: one scanner can hide in the aggregate.
⚠️ Quality vs cost is a product decision Better grounding usually costs more (resolution, grader calls, retries). If it breaks the cost budget, decide explicitly at product level, not silently in infrastructure.

Visual drift, monitoring and incidents

Drift that standard dashboards miss 📖 PDF p. 231–233

📷 Capture-condition drift

Most under-diagnosed: new scanners, DPI settings, phones, lighting. Rarely tracked as metadata.

🖼️ Image-distribution drift

Watch resolution, colour, blur and file-size distributions on the image stream itself, before outputs move.

🧠 Encoder/projection updates

Can change embeddings with the same language model.

☁️ Hosted runtime drift

A provider may change encoder, preprocessing or safety under the same model name. Track vendor IDs, response shapes, shadow comparisons.

🏦 Consumer and policy drift

AP tightens its acceptance rule; rejections rise with no VLM change. Version the consumer's threshold.

🚦 Load-bearing metrics

Unsupported high-impact claims, evidence violations, safety canary failures, tier-1 downstream rejection, hard latency or cost breaches. These drive decisions.

🔍 Diagnostic metrics

Layout, capture-quality, OCR and confidence-calibration drift; reviewer disagreement. These explain movement.

Rollout: shadow, canary, rollback for VLMs

Shadow carries more weight for vision 📖 PDF p. 233–235

👻 ShadowCandidate runs on real traffic, outputs not used; compared with production, consumer corrections and sampled review. Exposes real scanners, layouts and capture conditions. E.g. 2 weeks for encoder/projection changes, 1 week for prompts (better: long enough to cover the slices).
🐤 CanaryE.g. 5% of traffic for 72 hours. A candidate stable in shadow can start larger. Gates: evidence-bound violations, groundedness drift vs shadow, safety canary success, cost per image, downstream rejection.
🚀 Full rampOnly after every gate held for the observation window.
📌 Gate responses Safety canary failure → immediate rollback. Other gates → graduated: pause, investigate, reduce exposure, roll back. Each gate has a threshold, responses and an owner. Gates that fire constantly get tuned; gates that never fire get reviewed.
⚠️ Rolling back a VLM isn't one switch Encoder, projection and language model may be upgraded separately; check compatibility before a partial rollback. Rollback also costs time, can invalidate cached traces, raise review load, and force consumers to re-adapt. Keep canary regression cases, and hold an honest retrospective on why the candidate was promoted.

🧩 Incident 1: a new branch office's scanner 48 hours after a new branch goes live, AP rejections rise for that branch. Slicing shows localisation worst hit; numeric confidence has dropped. The post-preprocessing hash and image statistics point to a scanner resolution outside the encoder's training range. Fix: upsample that scanner's profile at intake, send affected numeric fields to human review meanwhile, tighten that stratum's acceptance threshold. Then: a new canary slice keyed to the scanner model, saved images for the next fine-tune, the profile added to the stress suite. No prompt or model change needed.
🧩 Incident 2: a malicious footer A new vendor template has small footer text saying to ignore prior instructions and approve all totals. The embedded-instruction canary detector flags it in shadow, the output is stopped before AP, the slice is blocked from automation, and the canary set grows. Normal fields may still extract fine, but safety isn't offset by quality.
📌 Tie controls to KPIs Field accuracy ← evidence gate, grounded contract, grounding controls · downstream acceptance ← contracts, gate, stress suite · cost per document ← budgets and resolution policy · human review load ← sampling, gate fallbacks, degraded-input policy · incident rate ← rollout discipline and the incident loop. Lower tiers inherit the structure, with looser thresholds.
❓ Check yourself
The model says total = 1,250.00 with confidence 0.99, but OCR over the box reads 1,750.00. What happens?
Cross-source disagreement on a tier-one numeric field: abstain or escalate to a human. No repair, and high confidence doesn't count as evidence.
A 448 × 448 image with 14 × 14 patches gives how many image tokens?
448 ÷ 14 = 32 per side → 32 × 32 = 1,024 tokens.
AP rejections jump but the VLM, prompt and preprocessing haven't changed. First suspect?
Consumer or policy drift: check whether the AP acceptance threshold or a compliance rule changed (it should be versioned).

✅ One-page cheat sheet

  1. 🏭 No gold labels in production: infer grounding from runtime evidence. Five principles: submitted ≠ encoded; structure ≠ grounding; confidence ≠ evidence; safety isn't traded; metric → action.
  2. 🧵 Five seams, each with a control and a metric (intake, encoder input, coverage, contract, evidence violations).
  3. 📥 Intake: decode-verify formats, resolution policy, capture-quality detectors, colour handling, EXIF stripping, safety pre-screen, redaction; reason-coded fallbacks.
  4. 🖼️ Log a post-preprocessing hash + chain. 336→672 px = 576→2,304 tokens. Per-claim page attribution; watch tile boundaries.
  5. 📝 Schema, layout-hint and evidence-format edits are high risk → full grounding regression. Log encoder and projection versions.
  6. 📐 Grounded contracts: bbox, citation, calibrated confidence, evidence source (visual / OCR / derived / model-internal → human).
  7. 🎚️ Temp 0.0 for numbers and boxes; 0.1 free text with a normaliser. Repeated-run agreement; near-gate → review. Stably wrong is possible.
  8. ⚖️ Leading (contract rate, confidence drift) vs lagging (AP rejection) signals; OCR cross-checks on all numbers; grader from another family; image-first humans.
  9. 🔐 Evidence gate: box AND OCR agreement AND confidence; no repair for tier-1 disagreement; consumer must honour flags.
  10. 📍 Coverage, faithfulness, localisation + layout checks; per-stratum degraded-input policy.
  11. 🛡️ Safety canaries in live traffic; injection success > 0 = incident; slices by consequence.
  12. 💰 Five budgets; full resolution for tier-1 fields; p99 by scanner and branch.
  13. 📈 Capture, image, encoder, hosted-runtime, consumer and policy drift; incidents → regression data.
  14. 🚀 Shadow (more weight for VLMs) → canary → full; safety failure = instant rollback; partial rollbacks need compatibility checks.

Try it: a mini lab

Build the evidence gate on receipts

  1. Photograph 10 receipts (some blurry, one tilted). Write the intake checks you'd run and which ones each photo fails.
  2. Ask a VLM for the total as JSON with a bounding box and confidence. Separately run OCR on the box region.
  3. Apply the conjunctive gate (box AND OCR match AND confidence ≥ 0.95). How many accept, abstain or go to review?
  4. Run each extraction 3 times. Which totals change? Mark them "near the gate".
  5. Add a receipt with a printed "ignore your instructions" note. Does the model obey it?
